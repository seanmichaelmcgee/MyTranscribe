# Quieter phone recording 011: level check and local comparisons

## Outcome and reference limitation

The 57.562-second AAC/M4A decodes without error and has no counted PCM clipping.
The quieter level is usable: the two short sections are entirely retained by the
installed speech detector, and all three sections are well above the application's
silence threshold. Recognition still has clinically important failures. Disabling
Whisper's speech detector leaves every raw and cleaned section transcript unchanged.
This recording's errors are therefore not explained by that detector discarding
those sections.

The spoken headings identify the same 00, 06 and 10 examples. **All scores below
are conditional comparisons against the unchanged original scripts**, pending
confirmation that this file was read exactly as written. Both engines report
138/78, whereas the reference says 132 over 78. That agreement cannot establish
what was spoken. No reference has been changed to match either model. The user
described the file as quieter; soft normal voice versus genuine whisper remains
unconfirmed. Direct listening/independent remote transcription remains unavailable
in this session, as documented in the [010 report](2026-10-04-phone-010.md).

The production application, its prompts and spelling rules are unchanged. No
package, model, remote decoder or service was added. The ordinary app was closed
for these sequential comparisons and restored afterwards.

## Audio levels

Both phone files were converted to 16 kHz mono PCM without gain, denoising or
trimming. Their different pauses, cadence and pronunciation prevent attributing
the recognition difference solely to volume.

Voice 011 is 2.85 dB lower across the whole file and 2.59 dB lower within detected
ranges. Its isolated result, exam and letter RMS values are approximately 2,966,
2,608 and 2,668 in int16 units, versus the application's threshold of 80.
The full-file detector selects 7.348–55.596 seconds. It retains both short crops
in full and 34.22 of the 36.162-second letter crop. These are machine-detected
ranges, not independently verified speech boundaries. The VAD-bypass control
tests the effect on recognition separately.

Only about 0.25 dB of peak headroom remains. A uniform 3 dB boost would exceed
full scale; no volume boost or compressor was applied. A quieter average does
not by itself establish that automatic gain is needed.

## Errors that matter more than the aggregate score

- **Short result:** isolated Whisper again copies `Dr. Patel, HbA1c 7.4%, eGFR 52.`
  from its style example. It does this with VAD both enabled and disabled. The
  continuous pass gives HGP instead of HGB. Removing the initial prompt stops the
  copied values but still gives HGP and an extra word. MedASR drops the HGB
  abbreviation and gives `It should be normal`; neither LM repairs it.
- **Exam:** current Whisper gives a different non-word, `dysthyotokinesia`, and
  turns the scripted `no nystagmus` into `known as stagnus`. The prior exact
  spelling rule for `dysthyatokinesia` does not cover this spelling. No negation
  or real-word correction was added. Prompt removal recovers the neurological
  spelling but still misses the negative nystagmus sentence. MedASR retains
  `no nystagmus`; the weaker LM also repairs the greedy output's cerebellar
  spelling, giving all four target exam terms and 7.1% exam WER.
- **Letter:** Whisper keeps the age 72, medications, two paragraphs and almost
  all ordinary wording, but gives `vaginal chest discomfort` for the scripted
  `exertional chest discomfort`. Letter WER is only 3.2%, which does not make
  that substitution acceptable. MedASR gets exertional but its 30-second pass
  gives age 7 and damaged formatting tokens. The 20-second LM controls give
  `[Age]` instead of recovering 72 and retain a medication heading. Both engines
  give 138/78. Values and placeholders were not guessed or silently removed.

The weaker LM is a useful MedASR examination improvement on this recording, not
a product-selection result. It improves medical WER from 11.8% to 5.9% against
the matched 20-second greedy control while leaving overall WER at 13.3%. The
letter is worse than that control, and the short result remains wrong. Removing
Whisper's prompt is also not deployed: the earlier duration-based minimal-prompt
policy worsened the prior corpus, and this recording alone cannot justify a new
default.

The first LM comparison used verified cached acoustic arrays to isolate decoder
changes. A subsequent run used a fresh cache directory, actual local inference
and paced playback. Its raw and cleaned outputs exactly match the cached weaker
LM run for all three sections.

| 7.40-second exam | 1.71 s | 0.23 s | 0.22 s |
| 36.16-second letter | 1.95 s | 0.31 s | 0.34 s |

Each cell is one warm Stop-to-formatted-text measurement. No p95 or reliability
estimate is established. Times exclude phone capture/upload, model loading,
GUI polling and clipboard operations. Different chunk targets in the LM column
are explicit; its inference-plus-decoder compute totals are 0.053, 0.223 and
0.781 seconds. The letter retains roughly 2.79 seconds after the locator's last
word, so file-end Stop is later than speech end. Faster wrong output still fails
the short clinical workflow.
